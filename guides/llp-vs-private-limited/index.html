LLP vs Private Limited Company: which one should you register?

The short answer

Choose an LLP if you are two or more partners running a service, trading or professional business, you want limited liability, and you plan to take most of the profit out. Choose a Private Limited Company if you plan to raise money from investors, give ESOPs to your team, or keep most of the profit in the business to grow. Both protect your personal assets. They differ in how profit is taxed when you take it out, how you can raise money, and how much compliance you face each year.

When an LLP is the better choice

  • You are two or more partners, for example consultants, agencies, traders or a professional practice.
  • You want your house and savings protected from business debts, which a partnership firm does not give you.
  • You plan to withdraw most of the profit each year. An LLP's profit is taxed once, and your share reaches you without further tax.
  • You want lighter yearly compliance. Below ₹40 lakh turnover and ₹25 lakh contribution, an LLP does not need a statutory audit.
  • You do not need shares, ESOPs or venture capital.

When a Private Limited Company is the better choice

  • You plan to raise money from angel investors or venture capital funds. They invest through shares, which only a company can issue.
  • You want to reward employees with ESOPs.
  • You plan to keep most of the profit in the business to grow. The company pays a lower rate than an LLP while profit stays inside.
  • Large clients, lenders or government buyers in your sector expect to deal with a company.
  • You can manage yearly compliance: audit, board meetings, an AGM and MCA returns.

How the tax difference works

An LLP pays tax at 30% (plus surcharge above ₹1 crore of income, and cess). When partners take their share of the profit out, it is not taxed again. Interest on capital and remuneration paid to working partners are deductible for the LLP within the limits in the Income-tax Act, and are taxed in the partners' hands.

A Private Limited Company that opts for the concessional regime pays 22%, about 25.17% with surcharge and cess. When it pays dividends, shareholders pay tax on them again at their own rates. Salaries paid to working directors are deductible for the company and taxed in the directors' hands.

Compare the total tax for your own profit and owners, including partner remuneration and director salary.

So, as a rule of thumb: taking most of the profit out favours an LLP; keeping it in the business favours a company. The real answer depends on your profit, how you pay yourselves and your personal tax slabs.

Yearly compliance compared

LLP

  • Form 11, the annual return, by 30 May
  • Form 8, the statement of accounts and solvency, by 30 October
  • Income tax return
  • Audit only above ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution
  • Yearly KYC of designated partners

Private Limited Company

  • Statutory audit every year; first auditor appointed within 30 days of incorporation
  • Board meetings during the year and an annual general meeting
  • AOC-4 and MGT-7 or MGT-7A after the AGM
  • Commencement of business declaration (INC-20A) within 180 days of incorporation
  • Income tax return, and yearly KYC of directors

Can you switch later?

Yes, in both directions, but it is not quick. A private company can convert into an LLP under the LLP Act, and an LLP can register as a company under the Companies Act. Each route needs consents from all owners, clearances, filings and fees, and the tax-neutral treatment applies only if conditions are met. It is far cheaper to choose correctly at the start.

Questions

Is an LLP cheaper than a Private Limited Company?

Setting up costs are broadly similar and depend on your contribution or share capital and your State's stamp duty. Running costs are usually lower for an LLP, mainly because a small LLP does not need a statutory audit and has fewer yearly filings.

Can an LLP raise money from investors?

An LLP can admit new partners who bring in capital, but it cannot issue shares, which is how most angel and venture investors invest. Foreign investment in an LLP is allowed only in sectors where 100% foreign investment is permitted under the automatic route without conditions.

Which is better for a startup?

If you plan to raise equity funding or offer ESOPs, a Private Limited Company. If you are bootstrapping a service business and taking profits out, an LLP is often the better fit. Both can apply for DPIIT startup recognition.

Can one person form an LLP or a Private Limited Company?

No. Both need at least two people. A single founder who wants limited liability can form a One Person Company.

Do I need a professional to register?

You can file online yourself with digital signatures, but some incorporation forms must be certified by a practising Chartered Accountant, Company Secretary or Cost Accountant.
