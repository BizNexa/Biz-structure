Business structure guide

Types of business structures in India: the 7 forms compared

The short answer

India has seven main forms of business organisation: sole proprietorship, partnership firm, limited liability partnership (LLP), one person company (OPC), private limited company, public limited company and Section 8 company (for non-profits). They differ in three ways that matter most: whether your personal assets are at risk, how the profit is taxed, and how much compliance you face each year. Most small businesses choose between a proprietorship, an LLP and a private limited company.

Each form of business organisation explained

1. Sole proprietorship

One person runs the business in their own name. There is no separate registration law: the business is identified through registrations such as GST, Udyam and Shops & Establishments. It is the quickest and cheapest way to start, but the owner is personally liable for every business debt.

2. Partnership firm

Two or more people share the profit under a partnership deed (Indian Partnership Act, 1932). Simple and inexpensive, with no MCA filings, but every partner is personally liable for the firm's debts, and each partner can bind the firm.

3. Limited liability partnership (LLP)

A partnership with a separate legal identity (LLP Act, 2008). Partners' liability is limited to what they agreed to contribute, and profit is taxed once. It suits professionals, agencies and service businesses that take most of the profit out.

4. One person company (OPC)

A company with a single member (Companies Act, 2013), with a nominee named in case the member dies or is unable to act. It gives a solo founder limited liability and a company's credibility, with company-level compliance.

5. Private limited company

The usual choice for startups and growing businesses. It can issue shares to investors and offer ESOPs, and the owners' liability is limited. It has the most structured yearly compliance of the common forms: statutory audit, board meetings, an AGM and MCA returns.

6. Public limited company

Needed to raise money from the public or to list on a stock exchange. It requires at least seven members and three directors, and faces the heaviest regulation.

7. Section 8 company

A company formed for charitable or not-for-profit objects. Profit cannot be distributed to members and must be used for the objects. It is the company form for NGOs and foundations.

How to choose the right structure

  • Starting alone with low risk? A sole proprietorship. Add limited liability later with an OPC or a private limited company.
  • Two or more people, taking most of the profit out? An LLP usually costs less tax and less compliance than a company.
  • Planning to raise investment or give ESOPs? A private limited company; investors invest through shares.
  • Risky sector, large contracts or employees? Choose a form with limited liability: LLP, OPC or private limited.
  • Charitable or non-profit purpose? A Section 8 company.

Can you change your structure later?

Yes. A proprietorship can move to a partnership or company, a partnership firm can convert into an LLP, and a private company and an LLP can convert into each other. Each conversion needs consents, filings and fees, and some tax benefits apply only if conditions are met. Choosing well at the start saves this cost.

Questions

What are the main forms of business organisation in India?

Sole proprietorship, partnership firm, limited liability partnership, one person company, private limited company, public limited company and Section 8 company. Hindu undivided families, trusts, societies and co-operatives are other forms used in specific situations.

Which business structure is best for a small business?

For one owner with low risk, a sole proprietorship. For two or more owners who want limited liability and take most of the profit out, an LLP. For a business that will raise investment, a private limited company.

Which structure has the lowest compliance?

A sole proprietorship, followed by a partnership firm. Neither files returns with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs.

Which structures give limited liability?

LLP, one person company, private limited company, public limited company and Section 8 company. In a proprietorship or partnership firm the owners' personal assets can be used to pay business debts.

Which structure pays the least tax?
