Advantages and disadvantages of an LLP in India

The short answer

An LLP gives partners limited liability and a separate legal identity, like a company, while keeping the flexibility and single taxation of a partnership: partners' share of profit is not taxed again. There is no minimum capital, and a small LLP needs no statutory audit. The main drawbacks: it cannot issue shares or ESOPs, which makes venture funding difficult; profit kept in the LLP is taxed at 30%, higher than a company's concessional rate; and late MCA filings attract a daily fee.

Advantages of an LLP

BenefitWhat it means for you
Limited liabilityYour liability is limited to the contribution you agreed. Your house and savings are protected from the LLP's debts.
Separate legal entityThe LLP owns property, signs contracts and sues in its own name, and continues when partners change.
Profit taxed onceThe LLP pays 30% (plus surcharge above ₹1 crore and cess). Your share of the profit reaches you without further tax.
Deductible pay to partnersRemuneration to working partners within legal limits, and interest on capital up to 12%, are deductible for the LLP and taxed at the partners' own slab rates.
No minimum capitalPartners agree any contribution in the LLP agreement.
Lighter compliance than a companyNo board meetings or AGM. Statutory audit only above ₹40 lakh turnover or ₹25 lakh contribution.
No limit on partnersUnlike a partnership firm (maximum 50), an LLP can have any number of partners.
Flexible managementThe LLP agreement sets roles, profit sharing and decision-making, without the formality of a board.

Disadvantages of an LLP

  • No shares or ESOPs. Angel and venture investors invest through shares, so fundraising is much harder than in a private limited company.
  • Higher tax on retained profit. Profit kept in the business is taxed at 30%, against about 25.17% for a company under the concessional regime.
  • No presumptive taxation. Unlike a proprietor or a partnership firm, an LLP cannot use the presumptive income scheme.
  • Late-filing fees. Form 11 (due 30 May) and Form 8 (due 30 October) attract an additional fee for every day of delay.
  • Two designated partners required, at least one resident in India, each with a DPIN and digital signature.
  • Less familiar to some lenders and large clients than a private limited company.

Who should choose an LLP?

  • Two or more partners running a professional practice, agency, consultancy or service business.
  • Owners who plan to take most of the profit out each year.
  • Businesses that want protection from personal liability without a company's compliance.
  • Not ideal if you plan to raise venture capital or reward staff with ESOPs: choose a private limited company.

Questions

What are the main benefits of an LLP?

Limited liability, a separate legal identity, profit taxed once, no minimum capital, deductible remuneration and interest to partners, and lighter compliance than a company.

What are the disadvantages of an LLP?

It cannot issue shares or ESOPs, retained profit is taxed at 30%, it cannot use presumptive taxation, and late MCA filings attract a daily fee.

Is an LLP better than a private limited company?

For partners who take most of the profit out and do not need investors, often yes. For businesses raising equity or offering ESOPs, a private limited company is better.

Does an LLP need an audit?

A statutory audit is needed only if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh. A tax audit applies above the income-tax turnover limits, as for any business.

How many partners can an LLP have?

At least two, with no upper limit. At least two must be designated partners, one of them resident in India.
