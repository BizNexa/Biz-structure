Private limited company: advantages and disadvantages

The short answer

A private limited company gives its owners limited liability, a separate legal identity and the ability to raise money by issuing shares and offer ESOPs, which is why startups choose it. Profit kept in the company is taxed at about 25.17% under the concessional regime. The trade-offs: a statutory audit every year, board meetings, an AGM and MCA returns, higher running costs, and dividends are taxed again in the shareholders' hands.

Advantages of a private limited company

BenefitWhat it means for you
Limited liabilityShareholders risk only any unpaid amount on their shares. Personal assets are protected.
Raise equity fundingThe company can issue shares to angel investors and venture funds, the way most startup funding works.
ESOPsReward and retain employees with stock options, which LLPs and firms cannot offer.
Lower tax on retained profitAbout 25.17% under the concessional regime (22% plus surcharge and cess), with no minimum alternate tax.
CredibilityBanks, large clients and government buyers are often more comfortable dealing with a company.
Perpetual successionThe company continues regardless of changes in shareholders or directors.
Easy change of ownershipOwnership moves by transferring shares, subject to the articles of association.
Deductible director salarySalary to working directors is an expense for the company and taxed in the directors' hands, with the salary standard deduction.

Disadvantages of a private limited company

  • Highest compliance of the common forms. Statutory audit every year, board meetings, an annual general meeting, and AOC-4 and MGT-7/7A filings.
  • Higher running cost for accounting, audit and company-secretarial work.
  • Dividends are taxed twice. The company pays tax, and shareholders pay tax again on dividends at their slab rates.
  • Post-incorporation steps. First auditor within 30 days, share certificates within two months, and the commencement-of-business declaration within 180 days.
  • Limits: at most 200 members, and the company cannot invite the public to subscribe to its shares.
  • Closing down takes longer and costs more than closing a proprietorship or firm.

Who should choose a private limited company?

  • Startups planning to raise investment or offer ESOPs.
  • Businesses that will keep most of the profit to grow.
  • Businesses where credibility with banks, large clients or government buyers matters.
  • If you will take most of the profit out and do not need investors, compare an LLP first.

Questions

What are the advantages of a private limited company?

Limited liability, the ability to raise equity and offer ESOPs, lower tax on retained profit under the concessional regime, credibility, perpetual succession and easy transfer of ownership.

What are the disadvantages of a private limited company?

Higher compliance and cost, a mandatory statutory audit, dividends taxed again in shareholders' hands, a 200-member limit and a slower closure process.

What is the tax rate for a private limited company?

About 25.17% under the concessional regime (22% plus a 10% surcharge and 4% cess), with no minimum alternate tax.

Is audit compulsory for a private limited company?

Yes. A company's accounts are audited every year, whatever its size.

Can one person form a private limited company?

No, it needs at least two members and two directors. A single founder can form a one person company instead.
