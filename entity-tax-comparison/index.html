Questions

Proprietorship vs LLP vs private limited: tax questions

Which business structure pays the least tax in India?

It depends on the profit, the number of owners, their other income and how much they take home. A proprietor pays slab rates and no tax up to ₹12 lakh of total income in the new regime. A firm or LLP pays 30%, but remuneration and interest to partners within the legal limits are deductible and taxed in the partners' hands at slab rates. A private limited company under the concessional regime pays about 25.17% on profit it keeps; dividends are taxed again in the shareholders' hands.

How is LLP profit taxed in FY 2026-27?

An LLP is taxed like a partnership firm: 30%, plus 12% surcharge where income exceeds ₹1 crore (with marginal relief), plus 4% cess. Remuneration to working partners within the section 35(e) limits and interest on capital up to 12% are deductible for the LLP and taxed as the partners' business income. A partner's share of profit is not taxed again.

What is the tax rate for a private limited company?

Under the concessional regime in section 200 a domestic company pays 22% plus a flat 10% surcharge and 4% cess, about 25.17%, with no minimum alternate tax. Under the normal provisions the rate is 25% (turnover up to ₹400 crore in tax year 2024-25) or 30%, with surcharge, and MAT at 14% of book profit.

Can an LLP use presumptive taxation?

No. Presumptive taxation for business and for specified professions under section 58 is available to resident individuals, HUFs (business only) and partnership firms other than limited liability partnerships.

Does a proprietor get the ₹12 lakh rebate?

Yes. A resident individual taxed under the new regime with total income up to ₹12 lakh gets a rebate of up to ₹60,000, with marginal relief just above ₹12 lakh. Business income gets no standard deduction; that applies only to salary.
