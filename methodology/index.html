How we decide

Biz Matrix recommends one of seven structures: Sole Proprietorship, Partnership Firm, LLP, One Person Company, Private Limited, Public Limited (unlisted) and Section 8 Company. The decision follows the BizMatrix Knowledge Base & Decision Rulebook in four steps.

  1. Hard rules first. Legal knock-out rules remove any structure the law does not allow for your answers, for example a partnership with one owner, or a company for a CA in practice.
  2. No match, or only one match. If no structure survives, we say so and explain the rule that removed the last one. If exactly one survives, it is shown as the “only eligible structure” with no score.
  3. Weighted scoring. Otherwise each eligible structure is scored 0–100 on nine factors. Weights add up to 100 and every weight is tagged by its basis: LAW (statute or rule), REG (regulator practice), MKT (market convention) or JDG (BizMatrix judgement). Weights are not law.
  4. Confidence and review flags. The margin between first and second sets the confidence band: 10+ points is clear, 5–9.9 is moderate, under 5 is a near-tie. Near-ties and any review flag show “Talk to a professional” instead of a confident verdict.

4. Tax model (factor F10)

Effective total tax on profit, entity plus owners, under the Income-tax Act 2025 for tax year 2026-27 (new regime; owners with no other income; 2 owners for firm, LLP and companies). The structure with the lowest rate among those eligible scores 10, minus 1 point for every 2 percentage points above it.

What this comparison simplifies. It uses three profit points (₹10 lakh, ₹1 crore and ₹5 crore). Companies are assumed to choose the 22% concessional rate (25.168% with surcharge and cess). It leaves out marginal relief at the ₹12 lakh rebate limit and at surcharge thresholds, the 15% cap on surcharge for dividend income, interest on partners’ capital, and any other income the owners have. It ranks structures; it is not a tax computation. For your own figures, use the Income Tax Calculator.

5. Quality control

The engine is tested against 25 published founder profiles on every release, and a release is blocked if any test fails. The rulebook is reviewed every quarter and immediately after any Finance Act or Companies/LLP amendment.
